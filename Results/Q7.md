# Q7: Attrition Risk Factors

## Business Question

Which identified employee risk factors are most strongly associated with attrition?

## Results

| Risk Factor | Employees With Risk | Attrition Cases | Attrition Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Overtime | 416 | 127 | 30.53% |
| Low Environment Satisfaction | 571 | 115 | 20.14% |
| Low Job Satisfaction | 569 | 112 | 19.68% |
| Low Pay | 370 | 72 | 19.46% |
| High Promotion Gap | 296 | 60 | 20.27% |

## Methodology Note

Five risk factors were evaluated using the definitions established in the earlier analyses: overtime, low environment satisfaction, low job satisfaction, low pay, and high promotion gap. Low pay represents the bottom pay quartile within each job role, while high promotion gap represents the highest tercile of the promotion gap ratio among employees with a non-zero promotion gap.

Each risk factor was represented as a binary flag indicating whether an employee met its defined condition. For each factor, the attrition rate was calculated as the number of employees who left among employees with that risk factor. The factors were then compared using both the number of attrition cases and the attrition rate.

## Key Finding

Overtime has the highest observed attrition rate among the five identified risk factors at 30.53%, with 127 attrition cases. The remaining four factors have relatively similar attrition rates, ranging from 19.46% for Low Pay to 20.27% for High Promotion Gap.

The ranking differs depending on whether attrition rate or absolute attrition cases are considered. Overtime produces the largest number of attrition cases because it combines the highest attrition rate with a relatively large exposed population. Low Environment Satisfaction and Low Job Satisfaction account for more attrition cases than High Promotion Gap despite having similar or slightly lower attrition rates, because more employees meet those risk conditions.

These results identify overtime as the strongest observed attrition indicator in this comparison by both attrition rate and number of attrition cases. However, the analysis is observational and does not establish that any individual risk factor causes attrition. The risk groups are also not mutually exclusive, meaning an employee can meet multiple risk-factor conditions.
