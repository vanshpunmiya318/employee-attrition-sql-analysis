# Q3: Overtime, Work-Life Balance and Attrition

## Business Question

How does work-life balance relate to attrition among employees who do and do not work overtime?

## Results

| Overtime | Work-Life Balance | Total Employees | Employees Left | Attrition Rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No | Poor | 58 | 15 | 25.86% |
| No | Fair | 240 | 26 | 10.83% |
| No | Good | 639 | 54 | 8.45% |
| No | Excellent | 117 | 15 | 12.82% |
| Yes | Poor | 22 | 10 | 45.45% |
| Yes | Fair | 104 | 32 | 30.77% |
| Yes | Good | 254 | 73 | 28.74% |
| Yes | Excellent | 36 | 12 | 33.33% |

## Key Finding

Employees who work overtime have a higher attrition rate than those who do not across every work-life-balance category. Among employees with poor work-life balance, attrition is 45.45% for overtime employees compared with 25.86% for non-overtime employees. The same pattern appears among employees with fair, good, and excellent work-life balance.

Within the overtime group, attrition is highest among employees with poor work-life balance at 45.45%. However, the relationship is not limited to poor work-life balance: even overtime employees reporting good work-life balance have an attrition rate of 28.74%, compared with 8.45% among their non-overtime counterparts.

Notably, work-life balance itself does not show a clean downward trend in attrition within either group. Attrition dips at "Good" and rises again at "Excellent" for both overtime and non-overtime employees. This means work-life balance alone does not cleanly predict attrition in this cross-tabulation, while the overtime difference remains consistent across all four categories.

The smallest groups should be interpreted cautiously, particularly overtime employees with poor (n=22) and excellent (n=36) work-life balance. Nevertheless, the consistent difference between overtime and non-overtime employees across all work-life-balance categories suggests that overtime is an important attrition indicator even when work-life balance is considered.
