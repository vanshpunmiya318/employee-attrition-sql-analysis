# Q6: Cumulative Attrition Risk

## Business Question

How does employee attrition change as multiple risk factors accumulate?

## Methodology Note

Risk factors are drawn from the pay and promotion-stagnation definitions validated in Q4 and Q5 (bottom pay quartile within role; top stagnation tercile among employees with a promotion gap), plus overtime and low job/environment satisfaction. Each employee's risk factor count is a simple sum of these five binary flags, not a weighted score.

## Results

| Risk Factors | Total Employees | Employees Left | Attrition Rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 216 | 10 | 4.63% |
| 1 | 560 | 70 | 12.50% |
| 2 | 468 | 83 | 17.74% |
| 3 | 184 | 50 | 27.17% |
| 4 | 36 | 20 | 55.56% |
| 5 | 6 | 4 | 66.67% |

## Key Finding

Attrition rises consistently as risk factors accumulate, with no reversals across all six levels, from 4.63% at zero risk factors to 27.17% at three. Combined, employees with four or more risk factors (n=42) show a 57.14% attrition rate, more than 12x the zero-risk baseline.

The five-risk group individually shows the highest rate (66.67%), but with only 6 employees this figure should be treated as directional rather than statistically stable. A single additional departure or retention would shift the rate by roughly 17 percentage points. The 4+ combined figure (n=42) provides a more informative summary than the five-risk subgroup alone, while still warranting caution because of its relatively small sample size.

Overall, the results show a clear monotonic association between the accumulation of these risk factors and employee attrition, with employees exposed to multiple risk factors experiencing substantially higher attrition rates.
