# Q2: Overtime and Attrition

## Business Question

How does overtime relate to employee attrition?

## Results

| Overtime | Total Employees | Employees Left | Attrition Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Yes | 416 | 127 | 30.53% |
| No | 1,054 | 110 | 10.44% |

## Key Finding

Employees who work overtime have a substantially higher attrition rate than employees who do not. The attrition rate among overtime employees is 30.53%, compared with 10.44% among employees who do not work overtime.

Although fewer employees work overtime (416 versus 1,054), the overtime group accounts for more employees who left (127 versus 110). This means overtime employees experience both a higher relative attrition rate and a higher absolute number of departures despite representing a much smaller portion of the workforce.

The difference in attrition rates is 20.09 percentage points, with overtime employees experiencing an attrition rate approximately 2.9 times that of non-overtime employees.

This establishes overtime as a strong attrition indicator and provides a basis for examining whether its relationship with attrition changes when considered alongside other employee risk factors (see Q6).
