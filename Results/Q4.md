# Q4: Pay Level and Attrition by Job Role

## Business Question

How does attrition differ between the lowest- and highest-paid employees within each job role?

## Results

| Job Role | Lowest Paid Employees | Lowest Paid Attrition (%) | Highest Paid Employees | Highest Paid Attrition (%) | Attrition Difference (Percentage Points) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Laboratory Technician | 65 | 32.31% | 64 | 14.06% | 18.25 |
| Research Scientist | 73 | 23.29% | 73 | 10.96% | 12.33 |
| Sales Executive | 82 | 12.20% | 81 | 22.22% | -10.02 |
| Healthcare Representative | 33 | 3.03% | 32 | 9.38% | -6.35 |
| Manufacturing Director | 37 | 8.11% | 36 | 11.11% | -3.00 |

## Methodology Note

Employees were divided into four pay quartiles separately within each job role based on MonthlyIncome. The analysis compares the lowest-paid quartile with the highest-paid quartile for each role. Therefore, the comparison reflects relative pay position within a role rather than absolute salary differences across the company.

Only job roles with at least 30 employees in both the lowest- and highest-paid quartiles were included.

## Key Finding

The relationship between relative pay level and attrition differs substantially by job role. Laboratory Technicians show the largest difference, with attrition 18.25 percentage points higher among the lowest-paid employees than the highest-paid employees. Research Scientists show a similar pattern, with a 12.33 percentage-point difference.

However, the pattern is reversed for the other three roles. Sales Executives have 10.02 percentage points higher attrition among the highest-paid employees, while Healthcare Representatives show a 6.35 percentage-point difference and Manufacturing Directors show only a small 3.00 percentage-point difference in the same direction.

The small difference observed for Manufacturing Directors contrasts with the much larger gaps seen in Laboratory Technicians and Research Scientists. Overall, these results indicate that the relationship between relative pay position and attrition varies by job role rather than following a consistent company-wide pattern.

These findings suggest that pay position should be considered in the context of the employee's job role rather than treated as a universal attrition indicator.
